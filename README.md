# Stream Ecosystem Model Version 2

**Stream Ecosystem Model Version 2** is a lotic (stream) ecosystem model,
updated to C# with .NET. It includes embedded and downloadable graphics and
interactive functions to change initial standing stocks and data in order to
parameterize the model for other sites.

## Overview

The program contains three versions of the lotic ecosystem model: the McIntire
and Colby Stream Ecosystem Model and two modified versions designed to
investigate the process of herbivory and the effects of irradiance, food
quality, and nutrients on the processes of primary production and grazing. With
each model the user can change initial values of state variables, parameters,
and input tables (e.g., the irradiance, nutrient, temperature, and flow
schedules, as well as the allochthonous introductions). Complete mathematical
documentation of the three versions is included in the model documentation.

The Stream Ecosystem Model was initially created by McIntire and Colby (1978).
Stream and riparian ecosystems are modeled as hierarchical systems of
biological processes, with physical and chemical processes expressed as driving
or control variables. Model structure incorporates abiotic processes in streams
as well as community dynamics (McIntire, 1983) and invertebrate functional
feeding groupings (McIntire, 1968, 1973; Cummins, 1974).

The model was developed for scientific purposes including generating
hypotheses, synthesizing field and lab research, and evaluating databases and
priorities for future research. It has also been used to understand temporal
dynamics of primary production and instream grazers, and for teaching about
interactions among instream trophic levels.

## Model Structure

The Stream Ecosystem Model has a hierarchical structure that represents dominant
biological processes in lotic ecosystems. Stream ecosystems are conceptualized
as coupled subsystems involving physical drivers, primary consumption by
invertebrates through grazing, shredding, and collecting, and microbial
decomposition of both autotrophic organisms and detritus. Predation by
invertebrates and vertebrates and processes related to the transfer of energy
among primary, secondary, and tertiary macroconsumers are also modeled.

## Model Versions

- **Base model** — the McIntire and Colby Stream Ecosystem Model.
- **Herbivory Version** — tracks the successional trajectory and production
  dynamics of the algal assemblage, as well as the response of grazers to
  corresponding changes in food quality and quantity. This representation also
  expresses the feedback control that grazing has on successional changes within
  the algal assemblage.
- **Riparian Version** — useful for investigating the effects of riparian zone
  canopy structure on processes in low-order streams. Photosynthesis and
  partitioning of primary production among three algal functional groups
  (diatoms, cyanobacteria, and chlorophytes) are modeled at an hourly
  resolution instead of daily.
